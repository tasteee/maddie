Spec 6

Chords panel — find, shape, drop

For producers, not theory students: no Roman numerals, no scale degrees. The panel makes new chords. A chord already in the editor is changed by the chord bar that appears on it. Hold any tile to hear it with every setting applied, then drag it in.

Principles

The rules every chords decision is checked against.

1Controls live next to what they changeThe crafting area shapes the chord you're about to drag in. The chord bar, on the selection, changes a placed chord. Nothing changes both.
2What you hear is what you dragA preview plays exactly what would be placed or changed: octave, inversion, voicing and pattern.
3Only preview what existsWith nothing selected, nothing plays. The UI says so right where you're looking.
4Same word, same scopeAll, Project and Favorites mean the same thing for chords and patterns.
5Space belongs to the projectSpace always plays the song, so you can hear a new chord in context. Previews are mouse-held.
6Nothing overlaps. The newest edit wins.Two notes on the same pitch, or two chords on a chord track, never share time. Whatever you just placed, moved or resized keeps its full size, and what it runs into makes room.

Drop on empty space · place

The normal drop. 1 bar long, unless the next chord starts sooner.

Bm
D
Fits ¾ bar Em7
  • While dragging, a ghost of the real notes follows the pointer, snapped to the grid.
  • New chords are 1 bar long. There's no length control: resize a chord in the grid by dragging its edge.
  • Auto-fit: if the next chord on that track starts less than 1 bar after the drop point, the new chord is cut to end exactly where that one starts. The ghost shows the fitted length, and the tag says how long it is ("Fits ¾ bar"). Nothing is ever overlapped or moved.
  • If the gap is smaller than one grid step, there's no room: the zone becomes that chord's replace or seam zone instead.
  • The start snaps to the grid division.
  • Releasing anywhere outside the grid cancels the drag.
  • Hold Alt to skip snapping. Auto-fit and the zones still apply, because nothing may overlap.
  • Only the left mouse button starts a drag. Known bug to fix: right-clicking a track tab currently starts dragging it sideways. A right-click must never move anything.

Drop on a chord · replace

The "body double." The new chord takes the old one's place and length.

Replace G Em7
  • Zone: the chord's body, more than 10px from either edge. The chord gets a 2px accent ring and a "Replace G" tag. Its notes fade so the ghost reads on top.
  • The dropped chord keeps the old start and length. It takes the panel's octave, inversion, voicing and pattern, because what you drag is what you get.
  • One undo step.
  • MIDI tracks: works on found chords (the ones the Project tab lists). The tag reads "Replace Em7 · 4 notes".

Drop on a seam · insert

Like dragging to reorder. Slip a chord between two that touch.

G

Insert · moves 3 chords
  • Zone: within 10px of a seam where two chords touch. A 3px accent caret marks the seam.
  • Everything after the seam on that track slides right by the new chord's length, shown as faded dashed outlines while you hover.
  • Only on chord tracks. In MIDI tracks a seam is just a place drop, because shifting loose notes isn't predictable.
  • One undo step undoes the insert and the shift together.

Chord bar · editing a placed chord

One fixed home: centered at the bottom of the grid, 12px above the scrollbar. It never chases the chord, so it always looks the same and never gets nudged off-center.

  • Shows when one chord is selected: a chord in a chord track, or a found chord in a MIDI track (selected here or from the Project tab). Hides with the selection.
  • Placement: horizontally centered on the grid (not the window), 12px above the horizontal scrollbar, on the floating surface with shadow-overlay. It slides up 8px and fades in over 140ms when a chord is selected.
  • The link to the chord is in the design, not the position: the selected chord gets a 2px ring in its track color, and its name label turns into a filled pill. The bar repeats the name and says where it is ("Minor · bar 1").
  • If the selected chord scrolls out of view, the bar stays. Clicking its name scrolls the chord back into view.
  • Its pattern picker opens centered above the bar (same width, 8px gap), so it never covers the chord bar it came from.
  • Changes are live and undoable. The pattern field opens the same pattern picker. There, holding a pattern plays this chord with it, and letting go applies it.
  • MIDI tracks: any change rebuilds that chord's notes from scratch, so the bar says "Replaces 4 notes". It never patches hand-edited notes.
  • Use in panel copies this chord's octave, inversion, voicing and pattern into the crafting area, then toasts "The panel now uses Bm's shape and pattern." It's the only bridge between the two, and it's always on purpose.
  • Esc deselects and hides the bar. Tab moves through its controls in order.

Library and Project tabs

Two tabs. Suggestions has its own rail.

  • All: every chord that fits the key, grouped by root note, starting from the key's root. Within a root: basic chords, then power, sus, 7ths, add, rich. Nothing hides behind a "More."
  • Search matches the chord name or plain name ("sus", "minor 7"). It clears when the panel reopens.
  • Type chips sit in one row that scrolls sideways: All · Basic · Power · Sus · 7ths · Add · Rich. You pick one at a time.
  • Group headers stick to the top while you scroll and show their count.
  • Project: one row per found chord: position (mono), name, track with its color dot. Ordered by position, all tracks mixed.
  • Clicking a Project row selects that chord in the editor, scrolls it into view, makes it the anchor for Suggestions and opens the chord bar.

Pattern picker

540 × 470 popover that floats out over the grid, so the panel stays visible.

  • Left column: All patterns · In this project · Favorites, then a divider, then the categories with counts.
  • Right column: the patterns. Each row has a check when applied, the name, a faint sub line (its category, or "used 3×"), a 16-step rhythm strip and a star.
  • Hold a pattern to hear it on the target chord. Let go to apply it. The picker stays open so you can keep trying.
  • The target is in the header: "Playing on Bm" (the selected tile) or "Changing Bm in Chords" (opened from the chord bar).
  • No tile selected: a notice reads "Pick a chord tile to hear patterns" and flashes if you hold a pattern. Letting go still sets the pattern, silently.
  • The star toggles Favorites anywhere it shows. Favorites are global. Its empty state reads "Star a pattern to keep it here, in every project."
  • Search covers all 1,024 patterns. Each category remembers where you scrolled. Esc or clicking outside closes it.

Zoom lives on the scrollbars

Replaces the D-pad. Each control sits on the edge it changes, so there's nothing to learn.

  • End of the horizontal scrollbar: − + for time, 25% per step, from 50% to 400%.
  • End of the vertical scrollbar: − + for row height, 2px per step, from 10 to 32px.
  • Corner: Fit. Time goes back to 100%, and row height is set so every visible row fits.
  • While zooming, a small readout ("Time 125% · Rows 20px") shows near the corner for 1.5s.

  • Buttons at a limit are disabled. Tooltips name the action and its gesture.

No overlaps (editor-wide)

Fixes today's bug, where notes stack and hide behind each other.

  • Applies to: placing, pasting, moving and resizing notes in MIDI tracks (same pitch), and chords in chord tracks (same time).
  • The note or chord you just touched keeps its full size. What it runs into adjusts:
  • One it fully covers is removed.
  • One it overlaps at the end gets its end trimmed back to where the new one starts.
  • One it overlaps at the start has its start pushed to where the new one ends.
  • One that surrounds it ends where the new one starts. The leftover tail is removed, not split off.
  • While dragging, notes about to be trimmed or removed show their new size as a faded outline, so nothing changes by surprise.
  • Everything adjusted by one action is one undo step.
  • Chord drops follow the same rule through auto-fit, replace and insert.

Toasts (editor-wide)

Inverted, in one fixed spot. This replaces Spec 5's bottom-center toast.

  • Look: inverted theme (a data-theme region: dark in a light app, light in a dark app), so a toast is easy to spot in a busy grid.
  • Position: top-center of the grid, 16px below the ruler. It floats above everything and never moves, so it can't collide with the chord bar at the bottom.
  • One at a time, replaced rather than stacked, 2.4s, paused on hover, role="status".
